What this change reworked

A summary stands for one exchange. A range used to start after its person’s last summary, so somebody who came back to a busy room drew one message standing for everything since — other people’s exchanges included, and the summaries already written for them. The range now starts at the question that opened the exchange. What a person missed while they were away is presence’s business, and docs/aide.md §15 already said an aide is not catch-up; §3 now says it in the mechanism rather than in prose. One open question in §16 closes with it: a first summary no longer reaches back as far as the record does.

A fold names the person it was written for. A race can still widen a range until it holds a summary written for somebody else, so a fold and the summary under it are no longer paired by position alone. render.ts pairs each folded message with the nearest summary that stands for it, and the marker reads ── 3 messages, summarised for priya below ──. Before this, a seat was told that the message under a marker stood for the messages above it, which was false wherever two ranges overlapped.

The room has a second completion signal. settled() reports the seats alone, which is the meaning rule 5 needs it to keep. quiet() resolves when no seat is taking a turn and no aide still owes a message, and a quiet event says the same to a listener. A host that wanted the one message a person reads used to count agent_start and agent_end against the aides’ own names; this run waits with one call.

An empty say is refused. The last run put a message with no text on the record: say took Type.String() and did not ask for a character in it. An empty message took a seq, rendered in every context after it, and counted toward the threshold that decides whether an exchange draws a summary at all. The tool now refuses it, the way an aide’s summarise always did.

The aide has its own file. session.ts held the record store, presence, the seats, the dispatch, the lock and the aide, at 1,380 lines. The aide is now aide.ts (319 lines): one class per person, a Draft that holds one turn’s state instead of the aide holding it between turns, and an AideRoom interface that names exactly what an aide is given of the room — the record, the lock, a model call, and a session to write into. turn.ts (65 lines) holds what a seat’s turn and an aide’s turn both need. session.ts is 1,162 lines and keeps what the room owns: what opens an exchange, what closes one, and who is owed a message.

Attention is the mechanism, and the words are shorthand for points on it. A seat's attention was a widening scale with two adjectives standing beside it rather than on it. seated(agent, attention) is now the general form and the scale's own vocabulary; passive(agent) and attentive(agent) are one line each over it, for the two points a room names often enough to be worth a word, and a bare agent still takes the default. The routing rule folds in with them: every message has a reach (named for a directed say, broadcast for anything else said, presence for somebody arriving or leaving), and a seat wakes when its attention is at least that wide, with a directed message additionally waking the one it names. Four ordered exceptions became one comparison, and the roster a seat reads describes the scale once rather than describing each seat's exception. Making that point settable while a room runs — which is what §12's rung 3 wants — is in FOLLOW_WORK.md.

An aide is a seat. It used to be a second kind of participant with its own machinery: its own agent construction, its own persistence, its own abort, its own session opener, its own scheduler and two counters beside the room's own. It is now seated when its person arrives, and it takes the turn every other agent takes. What makes it an aide is two pieces of data: it sits at none, the narrow end of the attention scale, so nothing said in the room wakes it and it cannot be addressed; and the close of its person's exchange wakes it with one tool in its hands, summarise, bound to the range it must stand for. Its turns land in <room>:<aide> like any seat's, the roster lists it as the seat it is, and §12's rung 3 — an aide that speaks while the room works — is now those two lines widened rather than a new mechanism.

The exchange is the room's, not the aide's. A question, and everything the room does until it goes quiet again, was private state inside session.ts that only an aide could see. It now has a file (exchange.ts, 84 lines), a type, a reader on the session (session.exchange()), and both edges on the event stream: exchange_opened when a person's question opens a round, and exchange_closed when the room goes quiet, carrying the range it turned out to hold. The order at the end of a round is fixed — settled, then exchange_closed, then whatever is written about it, then quiet. The comparison run below has no aide in it and still reports four rounds: that is the point. An aide is the first reader of a closed exchange and not the only one — a client folds the working under the question it answered, a host prices a round somebody asked for, and a later compactor stands over a stretch of them. The table of exchanges in this report is read off those events rather than worked back out of the record.

An aide is told to answer the question and stop. The runtime's own paragraph used to ask for the facts a colleague needs to act on, which is every fact the room raised. It now asks for the answer to the question, the facts that answer depends on, and what changed while the room worked — and it says to drop the rest, however true. The aide is handed its person's question on its own line, above the range, because that is what the one message has to answer. The three aides in workspace.ts lost a sentence of their own allowance each. The summaries in the run this report replaced averaged 158 words; here they average 95.
