27 activations produced 18 messages. Nine times a product read the whole room, decided the question was not its own, and ended its turn leaving no mark. The record shows what was said; the activation table is the only place the decisions to say nothing are visible at all.

Personas made the answers land on the right desk

Each person’s identity says what they act on. The products used it: the task list told Dan “T-130 is yours and it’s the only open item besides Priya’s inspection”, the time tracker opened to Sam with “before you put anyone on T-124 today”, and both went to Priya with the one thing needing her signature. Nobody routed those — each product matched what it knew against who was reading.

The blocking chain, not the calendar, produced the answer

Priya asked whether Thursday was safe to promise. No product answered from the date: the task list walked blocking_chain(T-118) down four links to the rebar delivery, materials read Hadley’s lead time, and the answer came back “Thursday isn’t expensive, it’s impossible — no amount of overtime buys it.” Richer product state changes the quality of the reasoning, not just the detail of it.

They kept working with the office empty

Priya left at message 8 without answering. Messages 9 to 14 are the three products settling what they could: re-dating the chain, hanging the operator ticket on the pump-hire task, and moving both deliveries rather than leaving a locked slot exposed. Two move_delivery writes happened with nobody watching.

The overtime request was never raised, and that is correct

The time tracker holds request_overtime and never called it. It repeatedly named the 44h-of-45h formwork position and put the decision to Dan, who approves spend, and to Priya, whose date it protects. A product with a write it declines to use because the call belongs to a person is the behaviour worth having.
